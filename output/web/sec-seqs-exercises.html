    1. Write down the first five terms of the sequence \(\{ x_n \}_{n=1}^\infty\) where \(x_n\) is given by
      🔗
      1. \(\displaystyle x_n = 2^{n-1},\)
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \(\displaystyle x_n = \frac{2n}{2n-1},\)
        🔗
        🔗
      3. \(\displaystyle x_n = (-1)^n \frac{n^2}{n!},\)
        🔗
        🔗
      4. \(\displaystyle x_n = \cos{\frac{n\pi}{4}}.\)
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗

    2. For each of the sequences in question 1 above write down a simplified expression for the term \(x_{2m+3}\text{.}\)

    3. Determine whether the sequence \(\{ x_n \}_{n=1}^\infty\) is increasing or decreasing, where \(x_n\) is given by
      🔗
      1. \(x_n = \frac{n}{2n+1}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \(x_n = \frac{10^n}{(2n)!}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      3. \(x_n = \frac{e^n}{(n+1)!}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      4. \(x_n = \frac{n}{2^n}\text{.}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗

    4. Consider the sequence \(\{ x_n \}_{n=1}^\infty\) determined by the following rules for \(x_n\text{.}\) Which ones are convergent? Determine the limit if one exists. Hint: l’Hospital’s rule can be used for the first two.
      🔗
      1. \(x_n = \frac{\text{ln } n}{2n}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \(x_n = n^2 e^{-n}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      3. \(x_n = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2n^2}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      4. \(x_n = \frac{2n^3}{n^2 - 1}\text{,}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      5. \(\displaystyle x_n = \left (1 - \frac{1}{2^n} \right ) .\)
        🔗
        🔗

    5. Use the Monotone Convergence Theorem to prove that the sequence \(\{ r_n\}\) converges where
      \begin{equation*} r_n = \frac{\prod\limits_{m=0}^{n} (2m+1)}{\prod\limits_{m=0}^n (2m+2)} = \frac{1 \cdot 3 \cdot 5 \cdot \dots \cdot (2n+1)}{2 \cdot 4 \cdot 6 \cdot \dots \cdot (2n+2)}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Prove that the sequence \(\{ a_n\}\) is divergent, where
      \begin{equation*} a_n = \frac{n!}{2^n} . \end{equation*}
      Hint: Show that the sequence is unbounded.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Consider the general arithmetic sequence \(\{ x_n \}_{n=1}^\infty\) where \(x_n = a + (n-1)d\text{.}\) Let \(S_m\) denote the sum of the first \(m\) elements of the sequence \(\{ x_n \}\text{.}\) Obtain a formula for \(S_m\) in terms of \(a,d,m\text{.}\) Hint: Consider the quantity \(2S_m\) as the sum of \(S_m\) with itself in the reverse order.
      🔗
      🔗
    8. Consider the general geometric sequence \(\{ x_n \}_{n=1}^\infty\) where \(x_n = ar^{n-1}\text{.}\) Let \(S_m\) denote the sum of the first \(m\) elements of the sequence \(\{ x_n \}\text{.}\) Obtain a formula for \(S_m\) in terms of \(a,r,m\text{.}\) Hint: Consider the quantity \(S_m - rS_m\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    9. Using the algebra of sequences theorem find the limit of the following convergent sequences.
      🔗
      1. \(\{a_n\}\) where \(a_n = \frac{(2n + 1) (n-2)}{n^3 + 2},\)
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \(\{ b_n \}\) where \(b_n = \frac{2}{7n^2} - \frac{3n}{n^2} + \frac{2n}{7n+1}\text{.}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
      Point out carefully the places where the theorem is being used.
      🔗
      🔗
    10. Consider the function \(f\) where
      \begin{equation*} f(x) = x^3 - 2 . \end{equation*}
      When Newton’s method for root finding is applied to \(f\) the sequence \(\{ c_n \}\) is produced where \(c_n\) is defined by the recurrence relation
      \begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} c_1 & = 1 ,\\ c_{n+1} &= \frac{2}{3} c_n + \frac{2}{3 c_n^2}. \end{aligned} \end{equation*}
      Newton’s method claims that \(\{ c_n \}\) should converge to \(2^{\frac{1}{3}}\text{,}\) the root of \(f\text{.}\) Use the algebra of sequences theorem to prove that: if \(\{ c_n \}\) has a non-zero limit then that limit equals \(2^{\frac{1}{3}}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗

        🔗
