    Section 2.5 Series exercises

    1. Investigate the convergence of the following series,
      🔗
      1. \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty (2n - 1) \, = \, 1 + 3 + 5 + 7 + 9 + \dots , \end{equation*}
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{1}{2^{n-1}} \, = \, 1 + \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{4} + \dfrac{1}{8} + \dfrac{1}{16} + \dots , \end{equation*}
        🔗
        🔗
      3. \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{1}{n(n+1)} \, = \, \dfrac{1}{2} + \dfrac{1}{6} + \dfrac{1}{12} + \frac{1}{20} + \dots. \end{equation*}
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    2. Using a diagram of the number line investigate the convergence of the following series,
      \begin{equation*} \text{(a) } \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n, \quad \text{(b) } \sum_{n=1}^\infty (-1)^n \left ( \dfrac{n+1}{n+2} \right ) . \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    3. The geometric series \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty ar^{n-1}\) has partial sums \(S_k\) given by
      \begin{equation*} S_k=\sum_{n=1}^k ar^{n-1} = \dfrac{a(1-r^k)}{1-r}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      Use this result to investigate the convergence of the series \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty n a r^{n-1}\text{.}\) Hint: Let \(T_k\) denote \(k^{th}\) partial sum of this series. Consider the quantity \((1-r)T_k\text{....}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    4. Recall from the lecture that the geometric series \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty ar^{n-1}\) has partial sums \(S_k\) given by
      \begin{equation*} S_k=\sum_{n=1}^k ar^{n-1} = \dfrac{a(1-r^k)}{1-r}. \end{equation*}
      Therefore the series is convergent only when \(|r|<1\text{,}\) in which case the infinite series sums to \(\tfrac{a}{1-r}\text{.}\)
      🔗
      For the following definitions of \(x_n\) determine whether the series \(\sum_{n=1}^\infty x_n\) converges and if so find the sum of the series.
      🔗
      1. \(x_n = \tfrac{2}{3} \left ( \tfrac{1}{2} \right )^n \tfrac{1}{4} \left ( \tfrac{2}{3} \right )^n\text{.}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      2. \(\displaystyle x_n =\left ( \tfrac{3}{2} \right )^{2n} \tfrac{1}{2} \left ( \tfrac{2}{3} \right )^{n+1}\)
        🔗
        🔗
      🔗
      🔗
    5. Consider the series
      \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \dfrac{2}{n^2 + 2n} . \end{equation*}
      Show that this is a convergent series by finding the partial fraction expansion of \(\tfrac{2}{n^2 + 2n}\) and examining the \(k^{th}\) partial sum \(S_k\text{.}\)
      🔗
      🔗
    6. Consider the infinite decimal expansion
      \begin{equation*} 2.104104104104 \dots. \end{equation*}
      By expressing this as a suitable geometric series find the rational number represented by the decimal expansion.
      🔗
      🔗
    7. Determine whether the following series is convergent or divergent
      \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{2n+1}{n(n+1)} . \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗

      🔗
      1. \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \frac{2}{n!} , \end{equation*}
        🔗

        🔗
        🔗
      3. \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty \left ( \frac{2n-1}{3+4n} \right )^n , \end{equation*}
        🔗

        🔗

    9. Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:
      \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty a_n , \quad \mbox{ where } a_n =\frac{2n^2 + 4}{3n^3 -2n}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    10. Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:
      \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty b_n \mbox{ where } b_n = \frac{2n+5}{\sqrt{n^6 +n}}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    11. Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:
      \begin{equation*} \sum_{n=1}^\infty c_n \mbox{ where } c_n = \frac{n!}{5^{n+2}}. \end{equation*}
      🔗
      🔗
    12. Determine whether the following series converges or diverges:
