Theorem 2.4.2. Uniqueness of limits.

If a sequence converges then its limit is unique, i.e. if \(x_n \to L\) and \(x_n \to M\) as \(n \to \infty\text{,}\) then \(L=M\text{.}\)
🔗
🔗

Proof.

Suppose that a sequence \(a_n\) converges to limits \(L , M \in \mathbb{R}\text{.}\) We use the technique of proof by contradiction. If we assume that \(L\) and \(M\) are distinct, i.e. that \(|L - M|>0\text{,}\) then we can let \(\epsilon = |L - M| / 2 > 0\text{.}\) Since \(a_n \to L\) there exists an \(N_1\) such that for every \(n \geq N_1\) we have \(|a_n - L| < \epsilon\text{.}\) Similarly, since \(a_n \to M\) there exists an \(N_2\) such that for every \(n \geq N_2\) we have \(|a_n - M| < \epsilon\text{.}\)
🔗
We wish to make use of both of these inequalities simultaneously. So we set \(N = \max(N_1, N_2)\text{.}\) Now for every \(n \geq N\) we can say
\begin{align*} |L - M|\amp = |L - a_n + a_n - M| \\ \amp \leq |L - a_n| + |a_n - M|, \text{ by the triangle inequality}\\ \amp \lt \epsilon + \epsilon \\ \amp = |L - M| / 2 + |L - M| / 2 \\ \amp = |L - M| \text{.} \end{align*}
🔗
This chain of relations has established that for all \(n \geq N\)
\begin{equation*} |L - M| < |L - M| . \end{equation*}
But this last statement is a clear contradiction as a real number cannot be strictly less than itself.
🔗
So we conclude that the assumption that led to this conclusion must itself be false, i.e. that \(L\) and \(M\) are indeed the same. This proves the result. ◻
