Definition 2.3.1. Convergence (informal).

Let \(L \in \R\text{.}\) A sequence \(\left \{ x_n \right \}_{n=1}^\infty\) converges to the limit \(L\) if and only if as \(n\) increases, the elements of the sequence get closer and closer to \(L\text{,}\) or equivalently, the distance \(| x_n - L |\) gets closer and closer to \(0\text{.}\)
🔗
Other notation and language used to say that the sequence \(\left \{ x_n \right \}\) converges to the limit \(L\) is to say that \(x_n\) tends to \(L\) as \(n\) tends to \(\infty\text{,}\) or in symbolic form
\begin{equation*} x_n \to L \text{ as } n \to \infty\text{,} \end{equation*}
or
\begin{equation*} \lim_{n \to \infty} x_n = L\text{,} \end{equation*}
where the latter expression is read as "the limit, as \(n\) tends to \(\infty\text{,}\) is \(L\)".
🔗
If a sequence is not convergent then we say that it is divergent.
